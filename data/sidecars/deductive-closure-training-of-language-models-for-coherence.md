  scope: Manual annotation of 100 implications and contradictions generated by Llama-2-7B

- id: proposition
  kind: result
  text: Deductive Closure Training is proved to strictly increase the probability a language
    model assigns to the correct answer for a question. The proof assumes self-generated seed
    question-answer pairs are correct with probability at least p*, and that conditioning
    on a correct related pair raises correct-answer probability by at least 1/p*.
  scope: A minimal unsupervised variant of DCT with a single implication per seed, no consistency
    check, and training to convergence on arbitrarily many seeds; the two assumptions are
    not empirically verified.
  evidence: Section 4 and Appendix E
- id: context-self-training
  kind: context
  text: Deductive Closure Training reframes inference-time consistency reasoning over implications
    and contradictions of a claim as a source of training-time supervision. It gives one framework
    for both unsupervised factuality improvement and supervised model editing.
  scope: Positioned as of publication at ACL Findings 2024 relative to inference-time belief-graph
    methods and to concurrent self-training work that ranks or filters self-generated data
    by model-assigned factuality labels rather than extrapolating implications.
  evidence: Section 2
- id: context-editing
  kind: context
  text: Deductive Closure Training is an entry point for readers asking whether model editing
    propagates to a fact's consequences rather than to the edited sentence alone. It is evaluated
    on multi-hop question answering and reversal-style generalization.
  scope: Demonstrated only on English-language datasets (CREAK, MQuAKE-CF, Reversal Curse)
    with a single Llama-2-7B model, and only with one layer of implications rather than a

key: akyurek2024dct
coined: DCT (Deductive Closure Training)
gloss: fine-tuning a language model on the implications of its own text that survive a self-consistency
  check
one_liner: Deductive Closure Training (DCT) prompts a language model to generate the implications
  and contradictions of seed text, keeps the most probable logically consistent subset, and
  fine-tunes on it — turning inference-time reasoning into training-time supervision for factuality
  and model updating.
terminology:
  Deductive closure: The complete set of statements that follow from a given collection of
    statements; a language model is deductively closed if it assigns high probability to a
    logically coherent set of assertions and to all their implications.
  Correlative implications: Implications generated after first prompting the model to list
    related background facts about the entities in a seed claim, so that the resulting statements
    combine the new information with the model's existing knowledge.
  Consistency evaluation: Selecting, among all assignments of True/False to a seed document
    and its generated implications and contradictions, the most probable assignment that respects
    every implication and contradiction.
  Transductive DCT: Applying Deductive Closure Training with unlabelled inputs from the evaluation
    set used as seed documents, so the model is updated with access to the test claims but
    not their labels.
  Double-checking: A filtering pass in which the language model is re-prompted to verify that
    a seed statement really does entail or contradict each generated statement, discarding
    those it does not confirm.
misreadings:
- 'Deductive Closure Training does not solve the Reversal Curse: with the same general-purpose
  implication prompt, reversed-direction accuracy on the person-to-description split rises
  only from 3.7% to 10.7%, and the paper describes the challenge as far from solved.'
- 'The accuracy gains are not simply the effect of fine-tuning on more self-generated text:
  removing the consistency-evaluation step drops CREAK accuracy from 83.5% to 77.5% with the
  identical 586 generated documents.'
- Most generated statements are not strict deductions; manual annotation found only 30–36%
  of MQuAKE generations to be strict logical implications, with the rest plausible-but-not-entailed
  text.
- The transductive CREAK result of 85.5% is not comparable to a standard train/test result,
  because it uses the unlabelled validation claims themselves as seed documents.
- 'The formal guarantee is not a general proof that self-training improves language models:
  it holds for a minimal single-implication variant without the consistency check, and only
  when two stated assumptions about seed correctness and prompt benefit are satisfied.'
- All results come from a single model, Llama-2-7B, and English-only datasets, so the reported
  gains are not evidence about larger models or other languages.
