  scope: Positioning as of the 2025 arXiv preprint; argued for myopic problems solvable by

  scope: Granite-3.1-8B-Instruct as solver in zero-shot; 5 epochs of LoRA (R=32, alpha=16,

one_liner: CRISP is a 122,000-plan multi-domain dataset of validated high-level plans for
  math and code problems, showing that a LoRA fine-tuned 8B planner writes better plans than
  a few-shot 70B model and cuts downstream error over chain-of-thought by up to 28%.
coined: CRISP
gloss: Complex Reasoning with Interpretable Step-based Plans — a training dataset of short
  high-level plans for math and code problems
key: vetzler2025crisp
terminology:
  high-level plan: A 3-5 step abstract statement of the logical strategy for solving a problem,
    with equations, function names and numerical computations deliberately omitted.
  myopic problem: A task solvable by a predefined sequence of steps, without adaptive decision-making
    or real-time environment feedback.
  planner and solver: In a plan-and-solve pipeline, the planner is the model that emits the
    high-level plan and the solver is the separate model that receives the plan plus the task
    description and produces the answer.
  extrinsic validation: Keeping a generated plan only if including it in the prompt yields
    more correct answers on the original task than solving without it, measured over 10 sampled
    solutions.
  Err down: Relative reduction in error rate compared with a Chain-of-Thought no-plan baseline
    using the same solver model.
misreadings:
- 'CRISP is a dataset of plans, not a reasoning model: the fine-tuned Granite-3.1-8B-Instruct
  planner emits plans that a separate solver model then executes, and the solvers are never
  fine-tuned.'
- Explicit plans do not always beat chain-of-thought. Plans from an untrained 8B planner raised
  error by up to 4.3% for a 70B solver, so the gains depend on plan quality rather than on
  planning per se.
- Fine-tuning an 8B planner does not close the gap to a large solver. Pairing a vanilla small
  planner with the 70B solver still outperforms the fine-tuned small planner with the 8B solver.
- The cross-domain transfer result is between math and coding-and-debugging only; the claim
  that planning ability generalizes to non-symbolic domains is stated as a belief in the paper,
  not measured.
- The 73.3% judge preference for fine-tuned plans is a pairwise LLM-as-judge score against
  Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct's own few-shot plans, with that same model acting as judge — not
  a human evaluation.
